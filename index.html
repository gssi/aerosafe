    Practical use

    How to apply Aerosafe on a project

    01

    Start from the contract and system context

    Identify the applicable ECSS baseline, review plan, AI/ML boundary, ODD, hazards, criticality rationale, action authority, interfaces, and compensating provisions.

    02

    Use Table 7 as an index—not as a substitute

    Select the assurance areas, then open the cited Appendix A items. Tailor each item explicitly; do not infer applicability from category labels alone.

    03

    Freeze the construction record

    Complete all ten fields and assign owners, gates, closure authority, links, and residual treatment before reviewing execution evidence.

    04

    Execute against controlled baselines

    Use configuration-controlled data, models, software, tools, target artefacts, tests, and claims. Record results and findings with bidirectional traceability.

    05

    Keep evidence ownership separate from closure

    The team producing evidence does not self-close independent findings. The designated project or customer authority closes, waives, rejects, or conditionally accepts.

    06

    Maintain the record after change

    Reopen affected controls when the ODD, data, model, target, software, hazard assumptions, or operating context changes.

    Industrial integration

    Why ECSS-ready matters

    Expressing AI-specific assurance through familiar ECSS-oriented roles, gates, configuration controls, and decision records may reduce the translation burden for organisations that already use ECSS processes. This is a design-level adoption advantage, not evidence of automatic compliance, reduced cost, or qualification success.
